# Menu Pricing Review — 2026-08-06

Suggested price changes for Ohana Belltown, based on two sources:

1. **Real competitor data** already gathered in the Competitor Pricing admin tool (19 nearby
   restaurants — mostly within 0.1–0.3 miles, several literally across the street — 41 price
   entries across 20 directly-comparable menu items). This is the strongest evidence in this
   review; every recommendation below cites its actual source(s).
2. **General judgment** on items with no direct local comparison data yet — flagged clearly as
   such, since it isn't backed by the same hard evidence.

**Confidence matters here.** A recommendation backed by 5 competitor data points is a lot more
solid than one backed by 1. Where the sample is thin (n=1), the suggested number is directional,
not precise — worth a second data point before actually changing the price.

Net effect if all changes are made: **cuts on the big entrees/apps, small raises on the
underpriced rolls and wings.** This isn't "raise everything" — it's fixing two real problems in
opposite directions: some flagship entrees are priced well outside what the immediate market
bears, while some of the everyday rolls are quietly underpriced against the exact same
competitors.

## Cut recommendations (currently overpriced vs. the immediate market)

### Ohana Cheese Burger — $26.00 → $22.00
**Why:** 8 competitor data points (the largest sample in this whole review) put the local average
at **$18.24**, range $13–24. At $26, Ohana's burger sits **42.5% above average** and is the single
highest-priced burger of any comparable found nearby. The description — "1/3 pound lean beef
patty, American/Swiss cheese, pesto mayo, pineapple, grilled onions, lettuce & tomato" — is a
solid, well-built burger, but nothing in it (patty size, cheese, toppings) reads as meaningfully
above what competitors are already offering at $18–24. $22 lands just above the top of the
observed competitor range rather than $2 above it, which keeps some premium-positioning room
without being the clear outlier on the block.

### Chicken Katsu — $28.00 → $22.00
**Why:** Only one direct data point ($18 at a comparable restaurant), so treat this as directional,
not final — worth checking one or two more nearby menus before committing. But $28 for a
description that's just "tender fillets, panko, fried, served w/ rice" (no stated side, no
premium cut) is high for the category on general market knowledge too: katsu is a comfort-food
staple, and $28 approaches steakhouse-entree territory for a dish that's fundamentally fried
chicken and rice. $22 is a meaningful cut but still leaves headroom above the one known comparable.

### Loco Moco — $28.00 → $22.00
**Why:** 2 competitor data points average **$18.98** ($18.95–$19). Loco moco is traditionally a
humble, affordable plate-lunch dish — Ohana's version is a fair one (beef patty, 2 eggs, gravy,
potato & mac salad side), but $28 is a 47.6% premium over what two nearby restaurants charge for
the same dish. $22 still prices it as one of the pricier items on the entree list (it has real
food cost: beef patty + 2 eggs + a full side), just not nearly 50% above the going local rate for
literally the same named dish.

### Edamame (Chibi) — $10.00 → $7.50
**Why:** Only one direct data point ($6.95), but this needs the least amount of evidence to act
on — edamame is boiled soybeans with salt, one of the lowest-food-cost items on any menu anywhere,
and $10 is unusually high for it by general market standards regardless of the single comparable.
$7.50 still leaves real margin on a near-zero-cost item while no longer being a visible outlier
next to a $6.95 nearby alternative (and there's still a $17 "Kahuna" large-portion tier above it
for guests who want more).

### Gyoza Yaki — $13.00 → $11.50
**Why:** 3 competitor data points average **$10.48** (range $8.45–$12). Ohana's gyoza is well
above all three. $11.50 sits at the top of the observed competitor range — recognizing it's a
made-from-scratch pork/cabbage dumpling with a real kitchen labor cost, not the cheapest gyoza
around, without being the most expensive one nearby either.

### Seaweed Salad — $10.00 → $8.50
**Why:** One data point ($7.95), but seaweed salad is a low-prep, low-cost starter industry-wide,
and $10 is high by general category norms as well as the one local comparable. $8.50 keeps a
healthy margin while closing most of the gap.

### Rainbow Roll — $20.00 → $19.00
**Why:** One data point ($18), a modest 11% gap. Rainbow Roll (California roll topped with 5
different fish) is a legitimately premium, ingredient-heavy roll, so keeping it above the single
known comparable is defensible — this is a small trim, not a correction.

## Raise recommendations (currently underpriced vs. the immediate market)

### Eel Roll — $11.00 → $13.50
**Why:** 2 competitor data points average **$15.50** (range $12–$19) — the widest and most
lopsided gap in the whole dataset (Ohana is 29% *below* average here, the mirror image of the
burger problem). Unagi (BBQ eel) has gotten genuinely more expensive as an ingredient across the
industry, which likely explains why every nearby comparable charges more for the same style of
roll. $13.50 is a meaningful correction while still landing below the average rather than jumping
straight to it — eel pricing varies a lot by portion, so a smaller first move makes sense before
matching the top of a $12–19 range.

### Chicken Wings (Spicy Teriyaki + Buffalo, same $15 price) — $15.00 → $16.50
**Why:** 5 competitor data points — a solid sample — average **$17.53** (range $11.80–$23).
Ohana's wings are 14.4% below average. $16.50 captures most of that gap without jumping straight
to the average, which is sensible given the range is wide (portion size/wing count likely varies
by competitor).

### Salmon Skin Roll — $8.00 → $9.50
**Why:** One data point ($10), a 20% gap — the largest percentage gap among the roll comparisons.
This is a well-built roll (crispy salmon skin, avocado, cucumber, tobiko, kaiware, garlic kewpie)
for a below-market price. $9.50 closes most of the gap while leaving it as the cheaper option next
to the one known comparable, rather than matching it exactly on a single data point.

### Tuna Roll & Salmon Roll — $8.50 → $9.00 (each)
**Why:** One data point each ($9.00), same modest ~6% gap on both. These are simple, base-level
rolls — small, round-number adjustment to close the gap. Keeping them priced identically to each
other (as they already are) makes sense; tuna and salmon are comparable ingredient costs.

### Spicy Salmon Roll — $10.50 → $11.00
**Why:** One data point ($11), 4.5% gap. Small round-number correction.

## Already well-positioned — no change recommended

- **California Roll** ($11 vs. $11.25 avg, n=2) and **Spicy Tuna Roll** ($10.50 vs. $10.75 avg,
  n=2) are both within about 2% of the local average — exactly where a commodity, comparison-shopped
  item should sit. These are the rolls a price-conscious diner is most likely to compare
  across menus, so staying close to market here matters more than on a specialty roll.
- **Dragon Roll** is an exact match to its one comparable ($20 vs. $20) — good validation that
  premium fusion-roll pricing is calibrated correctly at least in this one case.
- **Spider Roll** ($14 vs. $13, n=1) and **Poke Bowl** ($23 vs. $21.95, n=1) are both single-digit
  percentage premiums on dishes with real ingredient cost (tempura soft shell crab; tuna + snow
  crab mix) — a small premium here is reasonable positioning, not an outlier.
- **Takoyaki** ($9.50 vs. $8.99) and **French Fries** ($8 vs. $8.40) are both within 6% either
  direction — noise, not a signal worth acting on.

## Separate issue found: a real pricing math error (not a "which price is better" question)

**Yakisoba** (`'Aina Pa'ina (Entrees)`, currently $23 base) has a description that says *"With
chicken: $27 / YOSH $51.30"* — but the actual modifiers on the item are Add Chicken (+$4) and Yosh
Size (+$20.70):

- Base + chicken = $23 + $4 = **$27** ✓ matches the description
- Base + Yosh Size = $23 + $20.70 = **$43.70** ✗ description says $51.30 (off by $7.60)
- Base + chicken + Yosh Size = $23 + $4 + $20.70 = **$47.70** ✗ still doesn't match $51.30 (off by $3.60)

This was already flagged during an earlier description-cleanup pass and deliberately left
unresolved rather than guessed at, since it's not clear whether the **$51.30 in the description is
wrong**, or the **$20.70 Yosh Size modifier is wrong** (and the description is the actual intended
price). This isn't a "should we charge more or less" pricing call — it's a data-entry
inconsistency that's actively showing customers a wrong number on one side or the other, and is
worth resolving on its own regardless of anything else in this review.

## What this review does *not* cover

Only 20 of the menu's 245 items have real competitor data behind them — the Competitor Pricing
tool's dataset (19 nearby restaurants, 41 entries) happens to concentrate on rolls, a few pupu's,
and a few entrees. Everything else (nigiri & sashimi, teriyaki plates, bento boxes, all sake/wine/
beer/cocktails, combo plates, desserts, sides not listed above) has **no direct local pricing
evidence** in this review. I didn't extend recommendations to those by generic market guesswork —
that would be a much weaker basis for an actual price change than what's above. The Competitor
Pricing admin tool (`/competitor-pricing-admin.html`) already has the infrastructure to add more
restaurants/entries; the highest-value next step would be adding a few more comparison points for
categories not covered yet (especially nigiri/sashimi pairs and sake, since those make up a large
share of the menu) rather than guessing at prices without evidence.

## Methodology notes

- Competitor data pulled from the live Competitor Pricing admin tool (`/api/competitor-pricing/report`)
  on 2026-08-06 — restaurant list, prices, and distances were gathered by staff over recent weeks
  (`checkedAt` dates in the underlying entries range from 2026-07-30 to 2026-07-31), not scraped
  fresh for this review.
- "Meet in the middle" was used deliberately instead of jumping straight to the competitor average
  on larger gaps (burger, katsu, loco moco, eel roll, wings) — a 40%+ single-move price change is
  a real risk to regulars' expectations regardless of how well-justified it is on paper; smaller
  first moves are easier to make twice than to walk back once.
- Every suggested price lands on a round number or a clean `.50` — consistent with how the rest of
  the menu is already priced.
