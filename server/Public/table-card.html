Table Cards

Print this page (Cmd/Ctrl+P) to get table-ready cards sized for a standard 4×6" acrylic stand — one per table/seat across the dining room, bar, sushi bar, and deck, matching the real floor plan. Each QR code is unique to its spot: scanning the front lands on Happy Hour automatically during Happy Hour (Mon–Fri, 3–6pm) or the full menu the rest of the time, and unlocks an "Add to Order" button next to every menu item. A guest can tap that on as many dishes as they want, from any menu page, then tap the floating "Review & Send" button once they're ready — they see everything they picked, with a note that a staff member will come confirm it before it's sent to the kitchen, so nothing gets flagged for the kitchen until a real person has actually looked at it.

Laid out 2-up automatically — each printed sheet is one 8.5×11" page, landscape, with two full-size 4×6" cards side by side and a dashed line down the middle showing where to cut. The print dialog should switch to landscape on its own; double-check it did before printing if your browser/printer ever ignores that. A sharp paper cutter (most copy shops, FedEx Office included, have a self-service one) gets a cleaner edge than scissors.

Taking this to a print shop? Rather than printing straight from a shop's own kiosk browser, it's more reliable to make a PDF here first — Cmd/Ctrl+P, then choose "Save as PDF" as the destination — and bring that file in (on a USB drive, or uploaded through FedEx Office's own print-online portal). It carries the exact sizing with it instead of depending on whatever that particular machine has installed.

Each table also gets a gold-accented "Scan for Happy Hour" card — cut it off its sheet and glue it to the back of that same table's card so a guest can flip it over and jump straight to Happy Hour any time, instead of waiting for the automatic time-based switch above. It always shares a sheet with its own matching front card (never split across two different pages), specifically so the two halves to glue together come from the same cut.

The three cards at the top aren't tied to a table — "While You Wait" is meant for the host stand/front door: it shows the real menu with real prices so a waiting guest can browse, but doesn't unlock ordering (no table to deliver to). "Shop Ohana Swag" goes straight to the merch store, and "Join the Sushi Punch Card" goes straight to the Rewards page. Print those once; the table-specific cards below are the ones that need reprinting if the floor plan ever changes.
